Deep Dive Into an Idea

After getting an idea, you can start several activities that help you explore and understand it deeply.

1. Brainstorming
Generate as many ideas and solutions as possible.
Encourage everyone to share their thoughts.
Focus on quantity first, not perfection.
2. Documentation
Record ideas, research, decisions, and findings.
Make proper notes so important information is not lost.
Keep evidence and references for important decisions.
3. Stay Focused
Clearly define the problem you are trying to solve.
Keep the team focused on the user's actual needs.
4. Do Not Evaluate Too Early
During brainstorming, don't immediately judge or reject ideas.
First collect ideas, then evaluate them later.
5. Gather a Diverse Team
Include people with different skills, experiences, and perspectives.
For example: UX designers, developers, marketers, business people, and users.
6. Question the Obvious
Don't simply accept existing assumptions.
Ask "Why?", "Why not?", and "Is there a better way?"
Evaluate the Idea

An idea can be evaluated using three important criteria:

1. Feasible

Can we actually build or implement it?

Consider:

Technology
Resources
Skills
Time
Cost
2. Desirable

Do users actually want or need it?

Consider:

User problems
User expectations
User research
Usability
Demand
3. Viable

Can the idea work as a sustainable business or product?

Consider:

Business model
Revenue
Market
Competition
Long-term sustainability

A strong idea generally needs to be Feasible + Desirable + Viable.

Role of a UX Designer

A UX designer does not work alone. They often collaborate with:

Marketing – understanding customers and communicating the product.
Branding – maintaining a consistent brand identity and experience.
Developers – ensuring designs can actually be implemented.
Product/Business teams – balancing user needs with business goals.
Competitor Research
Identify Key Competitors

Find products or services that solve the same or similar problem.

Inform Your Design

Study competitors to understand:

What they do well
What they do poorly
Their features
Their user experience
Their strengths and weaknesses
Solve Usability Problems

Use competitor research and user research to identify problems and create a better experience.

Provide Reliable Evidence

Don't make design decisions only based on assumptions.

Use:

User research
Surveys
Interviews
Analytics
Competitor research
Usability testing
Make Proper Notes

Document:

Research findings
User problems
Competitor observations
Design decisions
Evidence supporting those decisions

In short:

Idea → Brainstorm → Research → Document → Competitor Analysis → Feasibility + Desirability + Viability → Design → Test → Improve
