LLM Sampling Parameters

These parameters control how an LLM chooses the next token/word from the available possibilities.


---

1. Temperature 🌡️

Temperature controls how random or predictable the output is.

Low temperature → more predictable and consistent

High temperature → more random and creative


Typical range

0–2 in many APIs, but the exact range depends on the model/API.

Temperature	Behavior	Best for

0–0.3	Very predictable	Code, maths, factual tasks
0.4–0.7	Balanced	General conversation
0.8–1.0	More varied	Creative writing
1.0–2.0	Highly random	Experimental/creative outputs


Simple example:

> Prompt: “The capital of France is…”



Low temperature → Paris almost consistently.

High temperature → the model has more freedom to consider less-probable choices.


---


2. Top-K Sampling

Top-K limits the model to only the K most probable tokens before choosing the next token.

For example:

Top-K = 5

The model considers only the 5 highest-probability tokens and ignores the rest.

Range

K is an integer, and commonly:

K ≥ 1

Some APIs also allow K = 0, which usually means Top-K is disabled.

Top-K	Effect

1	Most predictable; essentially chooses the highest-probability token
Small K	Less variety, more focused
Larger K	More possible choices, more variety
Very large K	More randomness/variety

The important idea is:

> Smaller K → fewer choices → more predictable
Larger K → more choices → more diverse




---

3. Top-P Sampling

Top-P (also called nucleus sampling) chooses from the smallest group of tokens whose combined probability reaches P.

Range

0 < P ≤ 1

For example:

Top-P = 0.9

The model keeps adding the most probable tokens until their combined probability reaches about 90%, then samples from that group.

Typical values

Top-P	Effect

0.1–0.3	Very focused/predictable
0.5–0.7	Moderate variety
0.8–0.95	Common balanced range
1.0	Considers the full probability distribution


Simple difference

Top-K:

> “Give me the best K number of tokens.”



Top-P:

> “Give me enough tokens to cover P amount of probability.”



So:

Top-K → fixed number of choices
Top-P → probability-based number of choices
